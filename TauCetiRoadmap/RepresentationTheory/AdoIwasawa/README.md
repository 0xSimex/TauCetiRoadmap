# The Ado–Iwasawa theorem and faithful finite-dimensional Lie representations

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field has a faithful finite-dimensional representation. In
characteristic zero this is Ado's theorem; Iwasawa removed the characteristic restriction. The result says
that an abstract finite-dimensional Lie algebra `L` can be embedded in the commutator Lie algebra of
`Module.End K V` for some finite-dimensional `K`-vector space `V`.

This roadmap builds the theorem as a reusable theory of finite-dimensional Lie representations. The
headline embedding is not the first target. The useful core is the construction of finite-dimensional
quotients of the universal enveloping algebra that still separate the canonical copy of `L`, together with
the extension machinery that makes such quotients equivariant under derivations. The summit is the
strengthened form proved by Hochschild: the faithful representation may be chosen so that every element
acting nilpotently in the adjoint representation acts nilpotently in the constructed representation.

The two characteristic regimes use genuinely different engines:

- in characteristic zero, build a faithful nilrepresentation on the center, extend it through a flag in the
  solvable radical by derivation-stable cofinite ideals of enveloping algebras, use Levi decomposition for
  the final extension, and add the adjoint representation to remove the remaining kernel;
- in characteristic `p`, construct central `p`-polynomials in the augmentation ideal of `U(L)`, make `U(L)`
  finite over the commutative algebra they generate, and apply the generalized Krull intersection theorem
  to a power of the central ideal.

These tracks meet only after each has produced the same faithful finite-target interface. A proof of the
characteristic-zero theorem must not be silently presented as the arbitrary-field result, and no use of
Lie's theorem, Levi decomposition, division by factorials, or characteristic-zero trace identities may enter
the positive-characteristic track.

## Scope, conventions, and the target

Throughout, `K` is a field, `L` is a Lie `K`-algebra, and `[FiniteDimensional K L]`. A representation is a
Mathlib Lie homomorphism

```lean
ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V
```

with `Module.End K V` carrying the commutator bracket. It is **faithful** precisely when `ρ` is injective,
equivalently when `LieModule.ker K L V = ⊥` after transporting the action to a `LieModule`. We retain the
explicit map in existence statements: an existential typeclass alone would hide the representation that
later consumers need to compose.

A **nilrepresentation** is a finite-dimensional representation on which every element of
`LieModule.maxNilpotentIdeal K L` acts nilpotently. The stronger Hochschild condition is pointwise:

```lean
∀ x : L, IsNilpotent (LieAlgebra.ad K L x) → IsNilpotent (ρ x)
```

It implies the nilrepresentation condition and gives a faithful nilrepresentation whenever `L` is
nilpotent. It is stronger than bare Ado–Iwasawa and is the final theorem of this roadmap.

The characteristic-`p` track uses the **augmentation map** `ε : U(L) →ₐ[K] K`, induced by the zero Lie
homomorphism, its kernel `U⁺(L)`, and the center

`C = Z(U(L)) ∩ U⁺(L)`.

Here `U(L) = UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L`. Central ideals generated by subsets of `C` are two-sided;
quotients are taken as associative `K`-algebras. Distinguish this `C` from the center `Z(L)` of the Lie
algebra.

The summit is:

```lean
theorem adoIwasawa [FiniteDimensional K L] :
    ∃ (V : Type u) (_ : AddCommGroup V) (_ : Module K V)
      (_ : FiniteDimensional K V) (ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V),
      Function.Injective ρ
```

and the strengthened theorem adds preservation of adjoint nilpotence. No algebraic-closure hypothesis and
no restriction on `ringChar K` appears in either statement.

## Why this is its own roadmap

[The Lie-groups roadmap](../LieGroups/README.md) needs the real case to prove Lie's third theorem by
embedding a real Lie algebra in `𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)`. [The highest-weight roadmap](../LieHighestWeight/README.md)
already owns PBW as a shared algebraic foundation, but its main theory is semisimple and characteristic
zero. Ado–Iwasawa needs neither highest weights nor classification; it needs cofinite quotients,
derivations, nilpotent and solvable ideals, and a separate positive-characteristic commutative-algebra
engine. Putting those targets in either neighboring roadmap would obscure their generality and make the
arbitrary-characteristic theorem appear to follow from characteristic-zero structure theory.

The original 1948 proof constructs finite-dimensional splitting algebras. This roadmap targets the theorem,
not that proof artifact. It follows the derivation-stable enveloping-algebra proof in characteristic zero and
Hochschild's central-ideal proof in positive characteristic because those routes expose broadly reusable
Mathlib APIs. A future splitting-algebra development can coexist with this one, but it is not a hidden
prerequisite here.

## What Mathlib and Tau Ceti already provide

- **Lie algebras, ideals, modules, and representations.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Basic.lean` and
  `OfAssociative.lean` provide `LieHom`, `LieIdeal`, `LieModule`, `LieModule.ker`, `LieModule.IsFaithful`,
  `Module.End`, and `LieAlgebra.ad`.
- **Nilpotence and solvability.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Nilpotent.lean` provides lower central series,
  `LieRing.IsNilpotent`, `LieModule.IsNilpotent`, `LieModule.maxNilpotentIdeal`, and the relation to
  nilpotent endomorphisms. `Solvable.lean` provides the derived series and `LieAlgebra.radical`.
- **Engel and Lie's theorems.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Engel.lean` and `LieTheorem.lean` provide the
  triangularization/nilpotence results used in the characteristic-zero track. Their exact hypotheses must
  be respected; Lie's theorem is not used in positive characteristic.
- **Derivations and semidirect sums.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Derivation/Basic.lean`,
  `AdjointAction/Derivation.lean`, and `SemiDirect.lean` provide `LieDerivation`, inner derivations, and
  semidirect Lie algebras. Mathlib's existing `Derivation` API is for commutative algebras; it has neither
  the required noncommutative associative-derivation structure nor a lift of a Lie derivation to `U(L)`.
- **The universal enveloping algebra.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping.lean` provides
  `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L`, the canonical Lie map `ι`, and the universal property `lift`. It does
  not currently prove that `ι` is injective, that `U(L)` is a domain or Noetherian for finite-dimensional
  `L`, or that it has the PBW ordered-monomial basis needed here.
- **Commutative algebra.** Mathlib already contains module-finiteness, Noetherian modules, integral
  dependence, and Krull intersection results in `Mathlib/RingTheory/Filtration.lean`, including
  `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff` and torsion-free consequences. The Tau Ceti implementation should
  generalize or adapt those results locally rather than reproving an Ado-specific intersection lemma.
- **Finite-target reductions.** The Etingof representation-theory formalization already states the
  arbitrary-field theorem and proves that it is equivalent to a finite-dimensional associative target of
  `U(L)` that remains injective on `L`; see
  [`Remark2_9_3.lean`](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/Etingof-RepresentationTheory-draft1/blob/master/EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter2/Remark2_9_3.lean).
  That project deliberately leaves the theorem itself outside its proof boundary. The reduction is useful
  design evidence, not a proof source to copy.

## The shared PBW dependency

The concrete PBW theorem is not in Mathlib. A 2025–2026
[Lean Zulip thread](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/287929-mathlib4/topic/The.20plan.20for.20PBW.20theorem/near/583326631)
records two active approaches: an abstract categorical proof and a Diamond-Lemma/normal-form proof.
[Mathlib PR #36936](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/36936) is an open draft of the
abstract categorical PBW theorem. Layer 1 uses any relevant API present in Tau Ceti's pinned Mathlib and
supplies the remaining concrete filtration, injectivity, ordered-monomial, and finiteness corollaries in the
shared Tau Ceti PBW development.

## What is missing

The missing library is substantially larger than the final existential theorem:

- PBW consequences for arbitrary fields: injectivity of `ι`, ordered monomials, filtrations, domains, and
  Noetherianity of `U(L)`;
- the augmentation ideal and lower-central-series-weighted PBW truncations;
- a reusable type for derivations of a noncommutative associative algebra, and extension of
  `LieDerivation K L L` to such a derivation of `U(L)`, functorially and compatibly with commutators;
- derivation-stable cofinite ideals in enveloping algebras of solvable Lie algebras;
- extension of nilrepresentations across split Lie-ideal extensions;
- the characteristic-zero structure lemmas needed by the extension argument, including the appropriate
  Levi decomposition and characteristicity of the nilradical;
- central `p`-polynomials in `U(L)`, module-finiteness over their commutative algebra, and the relevant
  generalized Krull intersection theorem for a finite torsion-free module;
- the strengthened Hochschild theorem and the characteristic-dispatch step that yields a theorem stated
  over an arbitrary field.

`Suggested.lean` pins only the load-bearing public interfaces. The definitive work below also includes the
supporting API and examples that make those interfaces usable.

## The build, in layers

### Layer 0: representations and finite enveloping targets

Build the interface before either proof track.

- **Faithfulness dictionary.** Prove that injectivity of `ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V`, triviality of its
  kernel, and `LieModule.IsFaithful K L V` are equivalent for the induced action. Supply constructors in
  both directions without installing surprising global instances.
- **Left-regular representation.** For a finite-dimensional associative `K`-algebra `A`, prove that
  `Algebra.lmul K A` is injective and that composing an injective Lie map `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ A` with left
  multiplication gives a faithful finite-dimensional representation.
- **Finite-target reduction.** If `q : U(L) →ₐ[K] A` and `q ∘ ι` is injective, obtain Ado's
  representation on `A`. Conversely, extend a faithful finite-dimensional representation along the
  universal property to `U(L) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`. State the equivalence.
- **Direct sums separate finitely many directions.** A finite direct sum of representations has kernel the
  intersection of their kernels. Deduce that if each nonzero direction is detected locally, finite
  dimensionality of `L` lets finitely many representations assemble into a faithful one. This is useful
  outside the selected proof as well.

Acceptance: the reduction specializes to the adjoint representation when `Z(L) = 0`; for an abelian
`n`-dimensional algebra, construct an explicit faithful square-zero representation as a sanity check.

### Layer 1: the PBW consequences Ado uses

This layer consumes the PBW project owned by
[the highest-weight roadmap](../LieHighestWeight/README.md). Put shared corollaries in the common Tau Ceti
PBW development rather than maintaining a second PBW theorem.

- **Canonical embedding.** Prove `Function.Injective (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι K)` over a field.
- **PBW filtration and ordered monomials.** Expose the degree filtration, its multiplicativity, the
  associated-graded equivalence with `SymmetricAlgebra K L`, and the ordered-monomial basis attached to a
  basis of `L`. The basis-free theorem and the concrete basis corollary are both required.
- **Functoriality.** Prove compatibility of the filtration and ordered-monomial spans with Lie maps,
  subalgebras, quotients, and direct sums. The later semidirect and central-polynomial arguments must not
  re-prove straightening identities by hand.
- **Domain and Noetherian corollaries.** For finite-dimensional `L`, prove that `U(L)` has no zero divisors
  and is left and right Noetherian. Route these through the associated graded symmetric algebra and
  filtered-to-graded transfer in reusable ring-theoretic form.
- **Finite spanning from monic relations.** If each member of a finite ordered generating basis satisfies a
  monic relation over a central commutative subalgebra `R`, prove that the bounded PBW monomials span
  `U(L)` over `R`. Package this as a module-finiteness theorem, not merely a dimension count inside the
  eventual proof.

Acceptance: for an abelian `L`, recover the polynomial/symmetric algebra basis and the familiar domain and
Noetherian instances. The statements must work over finite fields and in characteristics `2` and `3`.

### Layer 2: augmentation, weighted PBW truncations, and the nilpotent engine

- **Augmentation.** Define `ε : U(L) →ₐ[K] K` from the zero Lie map, prove `ε (ι x) = 0`, and develop its
  kernel `U⁺(L)` as a two-sided ideal. Prove its universal property among algebra maps killing `L`.
- **Powers and brackets.** Prove that a length-`n` iterated bracket maps into `(U⁺(L))^n`. State the exact
  indexing convention and test it on the Heisenberg algebra, where the central bracket belongs to the
  square of the augmentation ideal. Do not identify `L ∩ (U⁺(L))^n` with the lower central series unless
  a separately stated dimension-subalgebra theorem supplies the precise hypotheses, particularly in
  positive characteristic.
- **Weighted truncation for nilpotent algebras.** When `L` is nilpotent, choose a basis adapted to its lower
  central series and give each basis vector its lower-central weight. Use PBW straightening to prove that
  the span of ordered monomials of weight at least `N` is a left ideal, and choose `N` strictly larger than
  every basis weight. The quotient by that ideal is finite-dimensional, while the canonical copy of `L`
  remains injective.
- **Nilpotent action.** Left multiplication on the weighted quotient is a faithful representation. Prove
  that every `x : L` acts nilpotently: multiplication by an element of the augmentation ideal strictly
  raises weight until it reaches the truncated ideal. This is the characteristic-free Birkhoff form.

Acceptance: construct and compute the representation for the abelian and three-dimensional Heisenberg
Lie algebras. Check explicitly that the quotient action detects the center; the adjoint representation does
not.

## A. The characteristic-zero track

### Layer 3: derivations of enveloping algebras and stable cofinite ideals

- **Noncommutative derivations.** Introduce in Tau Ceti a structure for a `K`-linear endomorphism of a
  possibly noncommutative `K`-algebra satisfying `D (ab) = D(a)b + aD(b)`. Supply composition,
  commutator, descent to stable quotients, and the resulting Lie-algebra structure. Keep this distinct
  from Mathlib's commutative-algebra `Derivation` until that API itself is generalized to bimodules.
- **Lift Lie derivations.** For `D : LieDerivation K S S`, construct a noncommutative associative
  derivation `Dᵁ` of `U(S)`; prove `Dᵁ (ι x) = ι (D x)`, uniqueness on the algebra generated by `ι(S)`,
  linearity in `D`, and `[D₁,D₂]ᵁ = [D₁ᵁ,D₂ᵁ]`. Prove naturality under intertwining Lie maps.
- **Nilradical under derivations.** Over characteristic zero, build the structure theory showing that a
  derivation of a solvable finite-dimensional Lie algebra maps it into its nilradical. The reusable general
  statement is that `D(radical L)` is contained in a nilpotent ideal. Develop characteristicity of the
  radical and nilradical along the way.
- **Cofinite kernels.** Extend a finite-dimensional representation `σ` of a solvable `S` to an algebra map
  `U(S) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`; its kernel is a cofinite two-sided ideal. Relate nilpotence of `σ(x)` to
  nilpotence of `ι(x)` modulo that ideal.
- **Derivation-stable refinement.** Given a cofinite ideal `I` for which every nilradical element is
  nilpotent modulo `I`, construct `J ≤ I` with the same two properties and stable under every lifted
  derivation. Follow the standard construction: let `B/I` be the ideal generated by the nilradical image
  and take a sufficiently large power `J = B^m`. Prove finiteness of `U(S)/J` from PBW and monic
  relations, not from an implicit finite-generation claim.

Acceptance: prove the refinement is unchanged by replacing `I` with the kernel of an isomorphic
representation, and verify it for a two-dimensional nonabelian solvable Lie algebra.

### Layer 4: extending nilrepresentations across split ideal extensions

Let `S` be a solvable ideal of `L`, let `H` be a complementary Lie subalgebra, and suppose
`L = S ⋊ H` as a vector space and Lie algebra.

- **Multiplication-plus-derivation action.** Starting from a nilrepresentation `σ` of `S`, choose the
  derivation-stable cofinite ideal `J` from Layer 3. On `U(S)/J`, define `y + z : S ⊕ H` to act by left
  multiplication by `ι(y)` plus the descended derivation induced by `ad(z)|S`.
- **Representation law.** Prove the commutator identities among two multiplication operators, a
  multiplication and a lifted derivation, and two lifted derivations. Conclude that the construction is a
  Lie homomorphism `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K (U(S)/J)`.
- **Kernel control.** Prove `ker ρ ∩ S ≤ ker σ`. This is the invariant that permits iteration; do not replace
  it by an unproved assertion that `σ` itself extends on the same carrier.
- **Nilpotence control.** If the nilradicals of `S` and `L` agree, or if `L` is nilpotent, prove the extended
  representation remains a nilrepresentation. Separate the two arguments and state the hypotheses that
  make the latter's multiplication-plus-derivation sum nilpotent.
- **Functoriality under equivalence.** Transport the construction across equivalences of split extensions
  and across equivalent starting representations. Later clients should consume the extension theorem
  rather than its chosen vector-space complement.

Acceptance: extending the faithful square-zero representation of the center through a codimension-one
ideal preserves detection of that center.

### Layer 5: assemble Ado in characteristic zero

- **A faithful nilrepresentation of the center.** For a basis of `Z(L)`, construct commuting square-zero
  endomorphisms that detect every basis direction. Record a basis-free existence theorem and one explicit
  model.
- **Ideal flags.** Construct a finite flag from `Z(L)` through the nilradical and solvable radical in which
  each term is a codimension-one ideal of the next where required. Prove the structural lemmas that make
  the Layer 4 hypotheses hold at each step.
- **Levi decomposition.** Prove the characteristic-zero Levi decomposition in Mathlib's `LieIdeal`,
  `LieSubalgebra`, and `LieSemidirectSum` vocabulary: `L` is a semidirect product of its solvable radical
  with a semisimple complement. Include conjugacy/uniqueness facts only where they are needed to make the
  construction independent of choices; do not hide the existence theorem in an Ado-specific lemma.
- **Iterate kernel control.** Extend the center representation along the flag and then across the Levi
  complement. The resulting representation `ρ₀` satisfies `ker ρ₀ ∩ Z(L) = 0`.
- **Add the adjoint representation.** Since `ker(ad) = Z(L)`, the direct sum `ρ₀ ⊕ ad` is faithful. Prove
  `adoCharZero`, then prove the characteristic-zero case of Hochschild's stronger
  adjoint-nilpotence-preserving statement.

Acceptance: for centerless `L`, the construction reduces to `ad`; for nilpotent `L`, it agrees at the level
of guarantees with Layer 2 and remains a nilrepresentation.

## B. The positive-characteristic track

### Layer 6: central `p`-polynomials and module-finiteness

Assume `[CharP K p]` with `p` prime.

- **Frobenius commutator identity.** In an associative algebra of characteristic `p`, prove
  `ad(a^(p^e)) = ad(a)^(p^e)` with the exact interpretation of each `ad`. Build the supporting binomial
  and iterated-commutator API once.
- **Central `p`-polynomials.** For each `x : L`, finite-dimensionality of `L` makes the sequence
  `(ad x)^(p^e)` linearly dependent. Turn a monic linearized relation into
  `c_x = x^(p^e) + Σᵢ aᵢ x^(p^i) ∈ C`. The polynomial has zero constant term, so `c_x` lies in the
  augmentation ideal as well as the center. Record naturality under field extension and Lie maps.
- **The adjoint-nilpotent specialization.** If `ad x` is nilpotent, choose `e` with
  `x^(p^e) ∈ C`. This is the step that later forces `x` itself to act nilpotently.
- **Finite central generating family.** For a basis `x₁, …, x_d` of `L`, choose central `p`-polynomials
  `c₁, …, c_d` and let `R = K[c₁, …, c_d]`. Prove `R` is a commutative Noetherian `K`-algebra and,
  using PBW reduction of exponents, `U(L)` is a finite `R`-module.
- **Full central ideal.** Let `C = Z(U(L)) ∩ U⁺(L)` and `UC` be the ideal it generates. Left
  Noetherianity gives a finite central generating set for `UC`; enlarge it by the basis `p`-polynomials and
  retain module-finiteness over the resulting `R`. This full ideal, rather than only the chosen basis
  polynomials, is what yields Hochschild's strengthened nilpotence conclusion.

Acceptance: for a one-dimensional abelian algebra, obtain the familiar central relation in `K[x]`; for the
two-dimensional algebra `[x,y]=y`, compute central `p`-polynomials and verify the commutator identities in
characteristics `2` and `3`.

### Layer 7: the generalized Krull intersection and finite quotients

- **The module theorem.** State and prove the generalized Krull intersection result used by Hochschild:
  for a Noetherian commutative ring `R`, a finite `R`-module `M`, and an ideal `J`, an element of
  `⋂ₙ J^n M` is annihilated by some `1 + j` with `j ∈ J` (up to the library's sign convention). Reconcile
  it with the existing theorems in `RingTheory/Filtration.lean`.
- **Torsion-free/domain corollary.** Apply the theorem to `M = U(L)` over the central subalgebra `R`.
  PBW makes `U(L)` a domain, so multiplication by nonzero `1 + j` is injective; prove
  `⋂ₙ J^n U(L) = 0`. Check that `1 + j ≠ 0`: this uses `J ⊆ U⁺(L)`, hence augmentation sends `1 + j`
  to `1`.
- **Separate `L` at a finite stage.** The descending subspaces `L ∩ (UC)^n` have zero intersection. Since
  `L` is finite-dimensional, one is zero. Make the stabilization argument a reusable lemma about a
  finite-dimensional subspace meeting a filtered intersection.
- **Finite-dimensional quotient.** Prove `U(L)/(UC)^n` is finite-dimensional over `K`: it is finite over
  `R/J^n`, and the latter is spanned by finitely many monomials in the central generators of total degree
  `< n`. Expose an explicit finite spanning set.

Acceptance: every use of a commutative ideal occurs in `R`, and every ideal of the noncommutative `U(L)`
is explicitly two-sided. No coercion may make the Krull theorem appear to apply directly to a
noncommutative ideal.

### Layer 8: assemble the positive-characteristic theorem

- Choose `n` with `(UC)^n ∩ L = 0` and let `A = U(L)/(UC)^n`.
- The quotient map is injective on `ι(L)`, so Layer 0's left-regular construction gives a faithful
  finite-dimensional representation.
- If `ad x` is nilpotent, Layer 6 gives `x^(p^e) ∈ C`; a sufficiently large power belongs to `(UC)^n`, so
  `x` acts nilpotently on `A`. Prove the positive-characteristic Hochschild theorem and derive
  `adoCharP`.
- Prove independence from the selected basis and central generating family at the theorem level. The
  quotient itself need not be canonical, but its guarantees must not mention those choices.

Acceptance: instantiate the theorem over finite fields, including `𝔽₂`; no `CharZero`, `IsAlgClosed`, or
characteristic-size hypothesis may be inferable in the declaration.

### Layer 9: the arbitrary-field summit and consumers

- **Characteristic dispatch.** Use `ringChar K`. In the zero case install the local `CharZero K` instance
  and apply Layer 5; in the nonzero case use primality of the characteristic of a field to install
  `CharP K p` and apply Layer 8. Package this instance plumbing separately from the mathematics.
- **Hochschild's strengthened theorem.** Over an arbitrary field, produce a faithful finite-dimensional
  `ρ` preserving adjoint nilpotence. Derive the bare `adoIwasawa` theorem and the faithful
  nilrepresentation theorem for nilpotent `L`.
- **Matrix form.** Given a basis of `V`, transport `ρ` to an injective Lie homomorphism
  `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) K`. Conversely, show the matrix and endomorphism formulations are
  equivalent.
- **Lie's third theorem interface.** Export the real specialization in exactly the form consumed by
  [the Lie-groups roadmap](../LieGroups/README.md), without importing manifold theory into this roadmap.
- **Residual finite-dimensionality at degree one.** Record that the canonical copy of `L` in `U(L)` is
  separated by a finite-dimensional algebra quotient. Do not claim that all of `U(L)` is residually
  finite-dimensional unless that stronger theorem is proved independently.

Acceptance: the arbitrary-field declaration has only `[Field K]`, the Lie structures, and
`[FiniteDimensional K L]`; `#print axioms` in the implementation repository reports no project axiom or
placeholder for it.

## Ordering and parallel work

Layer 0 can begin immediately. Layer 1 uses the shared PBW work named in `LieHighestWeight`, consuming
whatever API is already available in Tau Ceti and its pinned Mathlib and implementing missing pieces in Tau
Ceti. Layer 2 consumes Layers 0–1 and provides a substantial first deliverable: the
arbitrary-characteristic nilpotent case by a weighted PBW quotient, independently of the central-ideal
construction used later in characteristic `p`.

After Layer 1, the two main tracks are independent. Layers 3–5 form the characteristic-zero track:
derivation lifts and stable ideals, split-extension machinery, then the radical/Levi assembly. Layers 6–8
form the positive-characteristic track: central `p`-polynomials, Krull intersection, then the finite
quotient. The commutative-algebra theorem in Layer 7 can proceed in parallel with the Lie-specific work of
Layer 6. Layer 9 needs both completed tracks only because its statement has no characteristic hypothesis;
the real consumer in `LieGroups` can use Layer 5 as soon as that track lands.

The largest risks are not the final compositions. They are concrete PBW infrastructure, the
derivation-stable ideal lemma, Levi decomposition at the right generality, and the clean separation of
commutative central-subalgebra ideals from two-sided ideals of `U(L)`. Claims should be cut along those
boundaries.
